Soultime is a mobile service platform built on time banking — where elderly users can request help, offer support, and accumulate service hours as a form of mutual care. A parallel family interface extends the system to health monitoring and coordination, without over-control.

Soultime
Time Banking App

Soultime begins from a clear gap: Taiwan's aging population is growing faster than its care infrastructure can respond, while most existing digital products do not fully address elderly users' social participation, service access, or interface comprehension. The project treats time banking not only as a service model, but as a design framework — one that turns mutual support, digital inclusion, and daily care into a single coherent system.

Aging Society

Taiwan's aging curve demands care systems that scale beyond institutions. This section maps the structural gaps — workforce shortage, social isolation, and the limits of passive, top-down support models.

A mutual support model where service hours become an exchange unit. Time banking reframes care as reciprocal rather than institutional — creating social value and practical support within communities.

Interview Insights

Sample

User Profile

15 interviewees in Northern Taiwan, aged 55 to 75. More than half had not previously participated in community service activities, but all had smartphone usage experience.

Usage Pattern

Digital Habits

Participants used smartphones for an average of 3.75 hours per day, mainly for news, articles, communication, and social media — indicating familiarity with simple mobile operations.

Need

Priority Functions

Users emphasized operation guidance, navigation to service locations, calendar reminders, trust-building mechanisms, and easy access to service records.

Research confirmed that designing for elderly users is not about simplification — it is about building trust, reducing uncertainty, and making contribution feel as natural as asking for help.

Design Criteria

Criteria
Hierarchy & Flow
Few page levels, clear function divisions, direct CTA patterns, and generous white space. Every screen should resolve in a single decision — users should never feel lost or need to backtrack.
Typography & Contrast
Solid type at approximately 20pt, strong background contrast, and simple wording. The option to adjust text size must be accessible from the first screen — not buried in settings.
Accessible Interaction
Large tap targets, rounded button shapes, minimal text input burden, and concrete icons paired with text labels. Abstract symbols are avoided to reduce cognitive load and support immediate recognition.

Design Strategy

Three directions shaped every design decision. First, time banking had to become legible — not as an abstract concept but as a visible flow of service requests, confirmations, hour accumulation, and feedback. Second, the dual-platform structure had to serve both autonomy and reassurance: elderly users needed agency; family members needed awareness.

Third, age-inclusive interface design was treated as a first-class constraint — not an afterthought. Guidance, readability, and emotional tone were built into the visual system from the start, making Soultime approachable for daily use without sacrificing functional depth.

A

Service Exchange

Translate time currency into understandable service records, hour accumulation, and exchange flow accessible to elderly users.

B

Family Coordination

Build a parallel family-side interface for activity tracking, health updates, and communication support without over-control.

C

Age-Inclusive Interaction

Lower decision friction through guided flows, large touch targets, and an information architecture that never asks more of the user than the task requires.

Logo Concept
The rotated S and M letterforms combine to create a hourglass silhouette — symbolising the cyclical nature of time and the reciprocal flow of mutual care. Blue, white, peach, and orange convey professionalism, respect, warmth, and community.
Typography
Avenir across six weights — light to black. The hierarchy supports both brand expression and in-app readability, with a body size baseline of 18–20pt and strong contrast against all background values.
Iconography
Concrete icons always paired with text labels. Consistent geometry, direct function reflection, and avoidance of abstract symbols — designed for recognition, not decoration.

Key Design
Decisions

Each decision traces back to a specific research finding. What follows is not a list of features — it is a record of how evidence was turned into design constraints.

Homepage
Entry Point
First Version Five homepage buttons — Request Help, Help Others, Calendar, Health, Badges. User testing showed participants paused over 3 seconds before tapping. Navigation felt like a menu, not an entry.
Final Decision Reduced to two primary actions. Every additional feature moves one layer deeper. The rule: the first screen should require no decision except direction.
Achievement
System
Considered A community leaderboard ranking participants by service hours. Research flagged this as a stressor — several interviewees said they would stop participating if they fell behind others.
Final Decision Individual badge system — Service Expert, Helping Hand, High Rating. Achievement is personal, not comparative. Contribution feels like its own reward, not a competition.
Family App
Boundaries
Initial Scope Full family messaging, direct service cancellation, and real-time push alerts for every activity update. Functionally complete, but the elderly user would feel constantly monitored.
Final Decision View-only: health status, activity records, location during service. No direct intervention. The design principle — awareness without control — protects autonomy on both sides.

App Interface & Functions

01

Login & Onboarding

Supports registration and account entry, followed by a simple health confirmation and visual onboarding sequence to strengthen recognition and emotional tone.

02

Homepage Hub

Provides direct access to request help, help others, calendar, emergency call, profile, navigation, and chat from the first layer of the system.

03

Request Help

Allows users to create new service requests, check progress, receive approval notifications, and add confirmed support to the schedule.

04

Help Others

Displays nearby support needs and enables users to join community assistance through application and matching flow.

05

Calendar & Reminders

Shows confirmed orders, activities, and reminders through year, month, and day views, supporting predictable time planning.

06

Badge System

Visualizes service expert, helping hand, and high-rating achievements to strengthen participation motivation and visible contribution.

07

Time Currency Records

Tracks service hours, transfer records, gifts, check-in, check-out, and redemption history as part of the time banking mechanism.

08

Navigation

Supports route guidance to activity and service locations, reducing uncertainty and external dependence during travel.

09

Health Status Record

Displays the latest record and historical summaries of mental, physical, and emotional conditions through simple chart-based feedback.

10

Chat & Family Contact

Creates direct communication between users and family or service-related contacts, supporting reassurance and coordination.

Soultime Family Functions

01

Status Overview

Allows family members to quickly review the elderly user's latest health entry, recent activity participation, and service status.

02

Location Support

Links the home view to map and location-related guidance so that family members can understand route context without directly interfering in activity flow.

03

Communication Window

Connects directly to chat so that reminders, check-ins, and emotional support can happen through a familiar conversation pattern.

04

Monitoring with Restraint

The family-side design is intentionally limited. It offers awareness — not surveillance. The elderly user's autonomy remains visible and intact within the system.

Outcome & Reflection

Soultime confirms that the combination of time banking logic and age-inclusive interface design can address both service access and social participation at once. The project does not simply create a care app — it proposes a digital environment where elderly users can request help, contribute time, connect with others, and remain visible within a mutual support network.

The central tension throughout was between simplicity and completeness. Every feature that made the system more capable also risked making it harder to use. Resolving that tension — through guided flows, layered disclosure, and a visual language calm enough for daily use — became the defining design challenge of the project.
