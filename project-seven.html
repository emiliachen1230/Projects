Project Overview

Pan’s Cake is a Taiwanese pastry brand rooted in gifting culture, where pineapple cakes function not only as food but also as carriers of trust, familiarity, and social meaning. As the brand expands into e-commerce, digital media, and broader retail contexts, its visual identity must perform more consistently across physical and digital environments. This project redesigns the brand system to improve clarity, flexibility, and recognition without losing its cultural warmth.

The Design Challenge

The existing identity carried a strong sense of tradition, but it lacked consistency across packaging, promotional materials, and digital contexts. As the brand reached wider audiences, the visual language became harder to scale and less effective for newer and more contemporary platforms. The challenge was to retain the brand’s sincerity and familiarity while introducing a more structured and adaptable system.

System Strategy

Balancing familiarity with a clearer structure

The redesign was built around a balance between cultural familiarity and structural clarity. Rather than replacing the brand’s traditional character, the system reorganizes it into a more legible framework through logo refinement, controlled color usage, supporting graphics, and a monthly visual program.

This project focuses on system-building rather than standalone visual outputs, treating branding as a framework that must remain readable, reusable, and consistent across changing contexts.

Key System Highlights

The identity system is built as a set of connected rules rather than isolated visual outputs. The highlights below summarize the core components that make the brand legible, flexible, and consistent across changing contexts.

Logo System
A primary logo and a compact version were developed to maintain clarity across both large-scale and small-scale applications. Protective zone, minimum size, improper usage, and background rules were defined to ensure consistent implementation.
Color and Typography
A controlled color identity and bilingual typographic structure were built to support readability, warmth, and visual continuity across print and digital outputs.
Supporting Graphics
Secondary graphic elements and style variants were designed to organize layouts, reinforce rhythm, and extend the identity without competing with the main logo. The logo geometry was also extended into supporting graphics inspired by Taiwanese floral tiles, allowing the system to carry local visual memory into broader applications.
Monthly Design System
The monthly design system combines seasonal color, botanical symbols, and seal-script characters into a time-based visual structure. The same logic then expands into twelve monthly compositions, turning one identity framework into a repeatable communication system for the entire year.

Monthly Visual Logic

The monthly design system is composed of three core elements: seasonal color, monthly botanical symbols, and seal-script characters. Seasonal color defines the temperature of time across the year, while each botanical symbol represents the natural rhythm of its month. The seal-script character functions as the structural core of each composition, anchoring the visual form and guiding its growth.

Usage Rules

  • The relative relationship between the three core elements must not be altered.
  • The seal-script character must not be used independently or removed from the composition.
  • Colors must strictly follow the palette assigned to each corresponding month.
  • The structure must not be rotated, distorted, scaled disproportionately, or reconstructed.

The twelve monthly compositions translate the system into a recurring communication format. Each piece keeps the same structural logic while shifting through seasonal color, botanical rhythm, and seal-script character, allowing the identity to evolve across the year without losing coherence.

Selected Applications

The identity was tested across the touchpoints that matter most for an actual pastry brand: product packaging, service materials, store-facing assets, and digital communication. Each application checks whether the system can stay recognisable while adapting to different scales, formats, and functions.

Process and Research

From review and comparison to the final identity logic

The project process began with a review of the existing identity and a comparison of market references, then moved into sketch exploration and logo refinement. These four stages established the strategic direction that later expanded into the monthly system and the wider application set.

03

04
