This kettle concept reframes an everyday countertop appliance as a premium ritual object. Developed with Georg Jensen-inspired formal restraint, polished metallic expression, and a clear interaction hierarchy, the project works as a recruitment-facing case study in brand translation, product storytelling, CMF thinking, and detail articulation from concept to near-prototype presentation.

Project Snapshot

What this project demonstrates

This case study shows how a recognizable luxury design language can be interpreted into a new appliance category without becoming a copy of existing products. The value of the project lies in how brand mood, user interaction, visual balance, and presentation quality are resolved together, allowing the kettle to read as both a functional tool and a desirable lifestyle object.

Role & FocusLed concept framing, form development, interface placement, detail refinement, and final visual presentation to communicate a premium countertop appliance with a coherent identity.
Design ChallengeTranslate Georg Jensen's sculptural elegance into an electric kettle that feels contemporary, usable, and brand-aligned instead of purely ornamental.
Process TranslationMoved from hero rendering and iterative sketches into product architecture, temperature controls, safety logic, and CMF options to make the concept legible as a real product proposal.
Outcome SignalHighlights the ability to connect research, aesthetics, interaction thinking, and polished visual communication in a way that is useful for product, industrial, and design-driven multidisciplinary teams.
01

Design Foundation

The project began with a simple design question: how can the sculptural calm and metallic refinement associated with Georg Jensen be translated into a kitchen appliance that still feels immediate in use. The final direction uses a softened taper, a pronounced handle-to-spout relationship, and a clean base-controlled interface to give the kettle a ceremonial presence while keeping the object operationally clear.

Product Positioning

Positioning

Design Purpose

The kettle is positioned as a premium countertop product for users who want utility to arrive with atmosphere. It is designed to support tea and hot water rituals while also behaving as a display-worthy object within a curated kitchen or studio environment.

Audience

User Profile

The intended user values quiet luxury, tactile refinement, and products that feel deliberate in both form and interaction. This includes design-conscious home users, boutique hospitality settings, and creative workspaces where appliances contribute to the visual tone of the room.

Context

Usage Context

The concept suits kitchen islands, tea corners, hospitality counters, and lounge spaces where a small appliance must remain elegant at rest. In these contexts, the kettle is expected to perform cleanly while reading as part of a broader interior language.

03

Design Objectives

Objective
Create an electric kettle with a distinctive premium identity while preserving operational clarity, safety, and everyday ease. The project is meant to show that appliance design can hold elegance and practicality in the same formal system.
Challenge
The central challenge is to avoid designing a beautiful shell with generic functionality hiding inside. Spout geometry, base controls, water visibility, and pouring balance therefore needed to reinforce the same premium narrative rather than feel like unrelated add-ons.
Core Features
Key features include a stable wireless base, visible water-level window, deliberate on/off toggle, current temperature feedback, and an adjustable heat range from 50°C to 100°C. Together these details frame the kettle as a controlled, hospitality-ready appliance rather than a basic household device.
Design Language
The design language is polished, restrained, and sculptural. Metallic surface treatment, curved transitions, and balanced proportion keep the object soft enough for domestic intimacy while sharp enough to feel aligned with a luxury brand vocabulary.
04

Key Design Decisions

The concept became convincing once the kettle was treated as a system of linked decisions rather than a single hero render. Three choices shaped the final direction most strongly: translating brand language into usable form, assigning the base as the control center, and making the pouring gesture feel intentional instead of merely decorative.

Decision 01

Turn brand cues into product geometry

Instead of quoting existing Georg Jensen products directly, the concept borrows a broader attitude of polished restraint, soft volume, and sculptural clarity. This allows the kettle to feel brand-adjacent while still operating as its own product category.

Decision 02

Place interaction on the base

Temperature selection, activation, and feedback are centered on the base so the main body can remain visually calm. This separation also improves legibility by giving the user one clear zone for control and one clear zone for handling and pouring.

Decision 03

Design the pour as a premium gesture

The handle arc and spout proportion are tuned to suggest controlled movement rather than speed alone. That shift matters because the project is not trying to celebrate boiling performance only, but the quality of the ritual surrounding it.

The process starts with a rendered hero study that establishes the kettle's silhouette, proportion, and formal attitude. 06

Final Proposal & Details

The final proposal consolidates the kettle as a complete countertop product rather than a single styling exercise. Body architecture, water visibility, temperature logic, certification cues, finish variation, and lifestyle positioning are brought together to show how the concept could move into a serious prototype conversation.

For a recruitment portfolio, this section matters because it demonstrates a capacity to think across object scale, interface logic, presentation fidelity, and the emotional reading of a product in context.

Pouring Experience

The kettle is designed so that the act of pouring feels controlled and deliberate rather than rushed. The body taper, handle reach, and spout line are composed to stage hot water transfer as a stable gesture, which helps the appliance feel premium at the point of use.

Compliance Awareness
Certification marking is included to suggest how the concept could begin to speak the language of real market products. Even in a speculative project, those cues help the proposal feel closer to a manufacturable appliance rather than a pure render exercise.
Controlled Pour Geometry

The handle is shaped to support multiple pouring angles while maintaining a composed wrist posture. This detail translates usability into visual character, letting ergonomic intent become part of the kettle's sculptural identity rather than a hidden engineering afterthought.

Countertop Positioning

In context, the kettle is intended to feel at home in spaces where small appliances are visible and therefore part of the interior narrative. Its polished body and restrained interface allow it to act as a countertop object with presence rather than a tool that needs to be hidden after use.

Recruitment Takeaway

This project demonstrates the ability to design beyond isolated form making. The strongest signal lies in translating brand cues into a new category, defining interaction through product architecture, and communicating the result with enough clarity and polish that the concept can be understood by design leads, collaborators, and potential clients at a glance.

Next Steps

The next phase would be to convert the current visual proposal into a validated appliance prototype, where ergonomics, material durability, heating performance, and interface clarity could be tested together rather than inferred from renders alone.

Prototype the Handling

Build a grip and weight mockup to verify wrist angle, pouring comfort, and balance when the kettle is filled to different water levels.

Refine the Interface

Test the base controls for tactile clarity, temperature readability, and accidental-touch prevention so the premium appearance remains matched by confident use.

Engineer the CMF

Translate the metallic finishes into a production-ready CMF package by evaluating coating durability, maintenance, heat resistance, and the relationship between reflective surfaces and daily wear.
