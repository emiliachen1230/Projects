Diving+ reorganizes fragmented dive-site information, course discovery, and community participation into a clearer decision journey for occasional and entry-level divers. As a recruitment-focused case, the project demonstrates UX research synthesis, information architecture, feature prioritization, and ecosystem-level product extension.

Project Snapshot

What this project demonstrates

Diving+ is positioned here as a recruitment-ready product case that moves from user research and market reading to information architecture, feature definition, interface direction, and service extension. The project demonstrates an ability to structure a fragmented leisure ecosystem into a coherent product experience while keeping the visual language approachable for younger and lower-frequency divers.

Problem FramingTranslated scattered dive information, trust barriers, and low-frequency participation into a clearer service opportunity with stronger decision support.
Design ContributionDefined the core product structure, dive-site browsing flow, feature priorities, and interaction direction for a mobile-first diving platform.
EvidenceUsed survey findings and journey-based analysis to prioritise legibility, confidence-building, and low-friction access to essential diving information.
Outcome SignalThis case demonstrates end-to-end thinking across UX research, interface design, visual system building, and concept-level product expansion.

Problem & Opportunity

The category opportunity came from a gap between enthusiasm and decision clarity. Diving is visually attractive and culturally rich, yet site information, participation routes, and trust signals are often scattered across stores, social channels, and word-of-mouth networks. Diving+ reframes that fragmentation as a product problem: how to help users evaluate where to go, whom to trust, and what to do next without overwhelming them.

Design Principles

The research suggested that the product should not compete on feature density alone. It needed a smaller set of strong product rules that could make browsing, evaluating, and committing feel easier for lower-frequency users while still supporting business-side visibility.

Reduce memory burden
Occasional divers should be able to re-enter the product without relearning terminology, flows, or site information from scratch.
Make trust visible
Store credibility, activity quality, and site conditions need to be legible early, because evaluation happens before users are willing to commit.
Connect discovery to action
Destination inspiration, courses, events, and participation paths should belong to the same ecosystem rather than separate browsing silos.

Research Insights

Research Translation

What the data means for product direction

The survey points toward a younger, lower-frequency audience, which changes the product brief. The real challenge is not to create more diving content, but to make information easier to trust, compare, and act on. That insight shaped the decision to foreground destination browsing, service clarity, and community-supported confidence instead of building a forum-first experience.

Research Questions

Questions that guided the interface decisions

  • How might occasional divers quickly judge whether a site or activity feels suitable and trustworthy?
  • What information reduces hesitation before booking a course or joining an event?
  • How can community content support decision-making instead of becoming noise?

Hardware Logic

The hardware logic view makes the concept legible as a designed object rather than a speculative sketch alone. It clarifies how display protection, input, recording, sealing, and core electronics could be organized into one wearable form.

  • Tempered glass front lens for underwater visibility and pressure resistance.
  • Photography module and camera lens integrated into the front body for quick capture.
  • Power switch, timer control, and recording module arranged around high-frequency actions.
  • Bluetooth module supports basic connection and data transfer to the Diving+ app.
  • Lens waterproof seal and sapphire crystal surface strengthen sealing and display protection.
  • Silicone eyeglass strap and silicone strap improve fit, retention, and long-wear comfort.
  • Diving mask frame houses the motherboard location and core electronics structure.

Design Decision

Strategic Framing Diving+ was shaped as a service ecosystem proposal rather than a single travel-booking interface, so each decision clarifies how the project shifts from fragmented dive information toward a more cultural, readable, and in-situ experience.
Decision 01
Discovery Language
BEFORE Dive services are often introduced through dense specifications, certification terms, and scattered destination listings. The first impression feels technical, cold, and difficult for beginners to enter.
AFTER The interface opens with an editorial feed, softer visual pacing, and lifestyle-oriented entry points so discovery begins from curiosity and atmosphere. The product therefore speaks in a more cultural and aspirational language before moving into operational detail.
Decision 02
Information Structure
BEFORE Dive site, course, booking, and record content usually live in separate pages or even separate services. Users have to build their own path mentally, which makes planning and comparison feel discontinuous.
AFTER Destination browsing, map reading, course selection, participation, and personal logs were reorganized into one sequential system. Instead of merely presenting screens, the project proposes a stepwise decision path from exploration to action.
Decision 03
System Extension
BEFORE Most case studies stop at the phone interface, leaving the actual underwater moment disconnected from the service logic. The app becomes the end of the experience rather than the bridge to it.
AFTER The wearable goggles concept keeps the same architecture alive inside the dive itself. Recording status, environmental cues, and display feedback move from mobile planning into underwater use, making the project read as a cross-device ecosystem instead of an isolated UI exercise.
